Know before you go

This edition of Good Outside carries the Good Outside catalog’s 2026-10-23 release: OpenBeta climbing places, OpenSkiData ski areas, Recreation.gov (RIDB) federal recreation areas and 49 curated regions, loaded for the area around your starting point. Coverage details and each source’s license are under Coverage. It is not an exhaustive inventory, and missing regions do not mean an activity is impossible. Areas within 450 km straight-line distance are candidates. Drive times are the road distance to each area’s gateway at a 60 mph average, as if on major highways the whole way, with no traffic, closures or slow final miles; areas over four hours are left out. When road distance is unavailable, straight-line distance × 1.3 stands in and the time is marked “est.” Biking and bikepacking match the same areas; neither confirms an overnight route or permission. Road gateways are approximate selected access points, not verified open trailheads. Activity tags indicate regional opportunities, not current openings or a verified itinerary.

Weather colors

Day trips (no camping) are rated on 8 am–6 pm each day: temperatures in that window, and rain, snow and gusts that fall within it. Overnight trips are rated on full days plus the nights between them. The worst day across the trip determines the label. Missing trip data stays Unranked. Excellent means the forecast meets our simple weather preferences; it is not an assessment of outdoor safety.

For hiking, backpacking, biking and bikepacking: Excellent generally means highs 8–28°C, gusts at most 35 km/h, precipitation at most 3 mm/day and rain chance at most 45%. Poor includes highs below 0°C or above 35°C, gusts above 65 km/h, snowfall above 1 cm, or rain at least 15 mm (10 mm for biking/bikepacking). Intermediate conditions are Passable.

Climbing is more sensitive to rain: above 0.5 mm is Passable; 3 mm or more is Poor. Rock drying, ice and rock type are not assessed.

Ski weather: Excellent has highs −10 to 3°C, gusts at most 35 km/h, and snowfall at most 10 cm/day. Highs outside −18 to 8°C, gusts above 65 km/h, snowfall above 25 cm, or more than 8 mm of precipitation with highs above 1°C are Poor. Other conditions are Passable. Snow base, lifts, terrain, rain/snow transition and avalanche danger are not verified.

For camping, hourly temperatures from 8 pm to 7 am between each trip day are included. Below 7°C is Passable and below 0°C is Poor, regardless of camping equipment. Permission, seasonal access and permits must be checked separately. No campsite inventory is queried.

Surfing

Surfing rates open coast, not individual breaks. Sample points every 20 km along Natural Earth coastlines are kept where open water reaches at least 150 km in most seaward directions, so sounds, straits and bays are left out; each is named after the nearest town. Each daylight hour scores on swell size (flat below about 1.6 ft, Poor above about 13 ft), swell period (10 s or more is good, under 7 s is wind swell), whether the swell direction reaches that shore, and wind (offshore or light is good, strong onshore is Poor). A day is its best three-hour window between 8 am and 6 pm (6 am–8 pm on overnight trips); the trip is its worst day. Tides and water temperature are shown, not rated. Marine forecasts come from the Open-Meteo Marine API (CC BY 4.0) about 8 km offshore and reach roughly a week to ten days ahead; wave models do not resolve nearshore shoaling, reefs or sandbars.

Elevation

Where an area’s terrain within 10 miles lies both below and above 3,000 ft (at least 8% in each), its details show two forecasts: one at a point near the median elevation of each band. Open-Meteo adjusts temperatures to each point’s elevation; precipitation comes from the weather model at that point. Snow level and freezing level are not assessed. Terrain comes from the elevation data credited below, at roughly 1 km resolution, so the highest summits read lower than they are.

Weather map, map detail & saved trips

After you choose a starting point, the map colours the land about 300 km around it by trip weather: an 8×8 grid of forecast points, each ranked with the same activity, dates, camping-night rules and thresholds as the area list, blended smoothly between points from red (Poor) through yellow (Passable) to green (Excellent). Places without a complete forecast stay uncoloured. Colour between grid points is interpolated, not forecast, and the grid is coarser than mountain weather; open an area or zoom in for point forecasts.

Zoom in to see nine weather samples in a 10-mile-radius circle. Every dot uses the same activity, dates, camping-night rules and color thresholds as the regional list. Today uses the full forecast day rather than only the hours remaining; these are forecasts, not real-time observations. These are forecast sample coordinates, not verified recreation sites, access points, trails, or campsites. Forecast models may be coarser than the spacing between dots.

The three suggested activity/area combinations use complete forecasts and modeled drives under four hours, one per larger region. Weather rating comes first, then shorter drive; your selected activity wins ties. Backcountry skiing is considered only when you select it. These are weather comparisons, not checks of skills, terrain or access.

Saved trips live only in this browser and retain their original dates. Forecasts are fetched again when reopened. Campsite searches open FreeCampsites.net at the selected coordinates. Its listings are not loaded or redistributed in this app; check each listing and the land manager separately.

How live data loads here

This copy runs in your browser. When you search, your browser asks Open-Meteo (forecasts, marine data and postcode search) and the OSRM demo routing server (drive distances) directly; they see your IP address and the coordinates being checked, and nothing else. The site that serves this page stores nothing about you: bookmarks and your last search stay in this browser’s local storage. Maps, place names and the catalog come with the page, so no other sites are contacted.

This page cannot contact other websites directly. When you open it in the Claude desktop app, it asks to use the app’s built-in browser, opens a tab there, and reads the forecast and routing services’ JSON answers from it. Claude may ask you to approve that and to allow each service’s site.

On a phone or in a web browser, signed in to Claude as the page’s owner, forecasts are fetched only when you search a starting location. The search starts a short Claude background task through the Claude Code Remote connector (Claude asks you to allow it the first time). The task fetches Open-Meteo forecasts for up to 30 nearby areas, the selected activity’s first, and saves them where only you can read them; the page shows them a minute or two later. Changing dates or camping reuses them; changing to an activity whose areas were not fetched, or tapping ↻, starts another task, and areas fetched within the last six hours are not fetched again. Saved forecasts are deleted after three days. Each search uses a little of your Claude usage. Drive times there are straight-line estimates marked “est.”. These saved forecasts carry whole-day values only, so day trips are rated on whole days there rather than 8 am–6 pm; postcode search, the weather map, zoomed-in samples, forecasts by elevation and surfing need the desktop app. For anyone other than the page’s owner, forecasts and drive times stay unknown and every region still shows with its source links.

To help fix problems, the page keeps a log of its last 60 live-data requests (service, number of points, time taken, and any error with the first characters of the page it read) in your private storage, where only you and Claude acting for you can read it. Error messages in it can name your starting point; it holds nothing else about you.
